What We’ll Work On, 2026–2029

Our work centers on three coalition-driven priority areas, supported by cross-cutting strategies and established community programs. Each goal below carries measurable targets and timelines in the full plan.

Priority Area 01

Housing

“United Is the Way Home.”
Housing Alliance of Harvey & Marion CountiesWith only 3.5% of our region’s homes truly available and 36.6% of renters cost-burdened, housing is one of the most urgent barriers to mobility. The Alliance unites government, nonprofit, and private partners to meet immediate needs and build long-term solutions.
  • Launch a coordinated home repair and modification program so aging, disabled, and vulnerable residents can stay safely at home — 10 repairs in year one.
  • Help add 20+ affordable housing units and put a new transitional or supportive-housing project in the pipeline by FY2029.
  • Publish an annual Housing Needs Report, reach 200+ neighbors a year through education, and grow a 15-organization advocacy network.
Priority Area 02

Health & Well-Being

Healthy Harvey Coalition & Marion County Health and Wellness Coalition
Changing conditions at the community levelChronic disease, food insecurity, and health disparities are deeply tied to poverty and place. We convene the Healthy Harvey Coalition directly and partner with the Marion County coalition to change conditions where people live.
  • Advance fresh-food retail in Hesston and support the start or expansion of at least 2 weekend meal programs for children and families.
  • Engage 300+ residents a year in wellness programming and deliver chronic-disease prevention reaching 150+ people annually.
  • Publish a Community Health Snapshot, reach 500+ neighbors a year with health education, and secure an active seat on the Marion County coalition.
Priority Area 03

Early Childhood & Child Care

“Strong Beginnings. Strong Communities.”
Harvey & Marion County Child Care Task ForcesKansas meets only 49% of childcare demand, and in Harvey County infant and preschool care can cost 31% of household income. These are workforce, economic, and equity challenges — and we’re building a system that works.
  • Contribute to the creation of 30+ new childcare slots in Harvey County by FY2029.
  • Build and distribute a local childcare resource guide for families and providers, updated at least twice a year.
  • Support 15+ new childcare workers, engage 10 employers on family-friendly policy, and pursue a formal partnership with the Kansas Office of Early Childhood.

Cross-Cutting Organizational Strategies

The coalition goals depend on a strong organizational foundation. These strategies build the capacity, partnerships, and infrastructure to deliver.

Resource Navigation

Grow client-centered, one-on-one navigation to serve 200+ neighbors a year, and launch a client-voice Advisory Council of people with lived experience.

Public Policy & Advocacy

Pursue a Rural Advocacy Fellowship, adopt a formal policy agenda, and produce 2+ data-driven policy products a year as a trusted voice on rural issues.

Partnerships & Community Hub

Deepen city and county partnerships, grow Community Investment Grants by 15%, launch a United Way Consulting Program, and build a lasting presence in Marion County.

Team Health & Sustainability

Strengthen internal collaboration and diversify revenue across giving, grants, corporate, and government sources so no single stream exceeds 40% of the budget.

Community Programs That Anchor the Hub

Three established programs round out our community hub function, building resilience, financial stability, and civic engagement.

VOAD
Voluntary Organizations Active in Disaster

Coordinating local disaster preparedness, response, and recovery — with quarterly partner meetings and annual training.

VITA
Volunteer Income Tax Assistance

Free tax preparation that returns real dollars to families, growing total returns filed by 10% year over year.

Volunteer United
Community Engagement Platform

Connecting 150+ volunteers a year with meaningful service across Harvey and Marion Counties.

How We Stay Accountable

A plan is only as good as the discipline behind it. We measure what matters — outcomes, not just activity.

Quarterly Reviews

The Executive Director reports to the Board each quarter on progress against SMART goals.

Annual Strategic Review

Each year, the Board reviews the full plan to adjust targets and respond to emerging needs.

Coalition Reporting

Every coalition tracks its own goals and reports to the Board at least twice a year.

Community Impact Report

We publish an annual report so neighbors, funders, and partners can see the progress.
